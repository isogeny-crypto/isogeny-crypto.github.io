FESTA

Fast Encryption from Supersingular Torsion Attacks

Published

June 18, 2026

Modified

June 18, 2026

1 Overview

FESTA is a public-key encryption (PKE) protocol that creatively repurposes the devastating attacks on the Supersingular Isogeny Diffie-Hellman (SIDH) protocol. While SIDH’s security was compromised by torsion-point attacks when auxiliary torsion information is revealed, FESTA relies on a novel trapdoor function where these very attacks are leveraged by the legitimate trapdoor holder to invert a one-way function.

By scaling the revealed torsion points with a secret matrix, FESTA prevents malicious attackers from utilizing the SIDH attacks [BaMaPo23]. Using standard transformations (like OAEP), this trapdoor mechanism enables the construction of an efficient IND-CCA secure PKE in the Quantum Random Oracle Model (QROM).

2 Protocol Design

The foundation of FESTA is an injective trapdoor function evaluated over supersingular elliptic curves.

2.1 Global Parameters

  • \(E_0\): A starting supersingular elliptic curve defined over \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\) (chosen with \(j(E_0) \neq 0, 1728\) to avoid non-trivial automorphisms).
  • \(\langle P_b, Q_b \rangle = E_0[2^b]\): A canonical basis for the \(2^b\)-torsion on \(E_0\).
  • \(\mathcal{M}_b\): A set of commutative, invertible diagonal matrices over \((\mathbb{Z}/2^b\mathbb{Z})^\times\) modulo \(\langle -I_2 \rangle\).

2.2 Setup and Key Generation

The trapdoor key generation works as follows:

  1. Compute a secret \(d_A\)-isogeny \(\varphi_A: E_0 \rightarrow E_A\).
  2. Sample a random matrix \(A \in \mathcal{M}_b\).
  3. Calculate the scaled torsion images on \(E_A\): \[ \begin{pmatrix} R_A \\ S_A \end{pmatrix} = A \begin{pmatrix} \varphi_A(P_b) \\ \varphi_A(Q_b) \end{pmatrix} \]
  • Secret Key (Trapdoor): \((A, \varphi_A)\).
  • Public Key: \((E_A, R_A, S_A)\).

2.3 Function Evaluation (Forward Direction)

To evaluate the one-way function on an input consisting of two isogenies \(\varphi_1: E_0 \rightarrow E_1\) (degree \(d_1\)), \(\varphi_2: E_A \rightarrow E_2\) (degree \(d_2\)), and a matrix \(B \in \mathcal{M}_b\):

  1. Compute \(\varphi_1: E_0 \rightarrow E_1\) and \(\varphi_2: E_A \rightarrow E_2\).
  2. Scale the torsion basis images under \(\varphi_1\) by \(B\): \[ \begin{pmatrix} R_1 \\ S_1 \end{pmatrix} = B \begin{pmatrix} \varphi_1(P_b) \\ \varphi_1(Q_b) \end{pmatrix} \]
  3. Scale the public torsion parameters under \(\varphi_2\) by \(B\): \[ \begin{pmatrix} R_2 \\ S_2 \end{pmatrix} = B \begin{pmatrix} \varphi_2(R_A) \\ \varphi_2(S_A) \end{pmatrix} \]
  • Output: The tuple \((E_1, R_1, S_1, E_2, R_2, S_2)\).

Without the knowledge of \(A\), the scaling by \(B\) (and natively \(A\)) thoroughly obfuscates the torsion point information, blocking the SIDH attack machinery.

2.4 Trapdoor Inversion

The trapdoor owner can invert the function by utilizing the commutativity of matrices \(A\) and \(B\):

  1. Undo the secret scaling on \(E_2\) by applying \(d_1 A^{-1}\): \[ \begin{pmatrix} R_2' \\ S_2' \end{pmatrix} = d_1 A^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} R_2 \\ S_2 \end{pmatrix} \] Because \(A\) and \(B\) commute, this effectively isolates the scaling to just \(B\), bridging the torsion mappings between \(E_1\) and \(E_2\).
  2. The resulting points reflect the exact unscaled images of \(R_1, S_1\) under the bridging isogeny \(\psi := \varphi_2 \circ \varphi_A \circ \hat{\varphi}_1\).
  3. Invoke an arbitrary torsion-point attack (e.g., Kani’s criterion generalizing to higher dimensions via (2,2)-isogenies) on \((E_1, R_1, S_1, E_2, R_2', S_2')\) to efficiently compute \(\psi\).
  4. Reconstruct the original kernels of \(\varphi_1, \varphi_2\) and the matrix \(B\) from \(\psi\).

3 Security Assumptions

FESTA introduces generalized hardness assumptions for the scaled-torsion setting:

  • Decisional Isogeny with Scaled-Torsion (DIST): Distinguishing a curve and its scaled torsion images (via matrix \(A\)) from a random supersingular curve and random basis points.
  • Computational Isogeny with Scaled-Torsion (CIST): Extracting the isogeny \(\varphi\) given \(E_0, E_1\), the domain basis, and the scaled target basis \(A(\varphi(P_0), \varphi(Q_0))^T\).
  • Computational Isogeny with Double Scaled-Torsion (CIST 2): Recovering two distinct isogenies \(\varphi, \varphi'\) given two instances of scaled target bases that use the same scaling matrix \(A\). Theorem 9 proves FESTA’s one-wayness reduces to the DIST and CIST 2 assumptions.
