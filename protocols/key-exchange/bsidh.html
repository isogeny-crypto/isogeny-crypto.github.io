B-SIDH

Supersingular Isogeny Diffie-Hellman using Twisted Torsion

Published

June 23, 2026

Modified

July 7, 2026

1 Overview

Broken by a polynomial time classical algorithm.

B-SIDH (pronounced “B-side”) is a novel instantiation that significantly improves upon the original SIDH protocol. It bypasses previous restrictions on the shape of the underlying prime field by permitting parties to operate simultaneously within the \((p+1)\)-torsion of a supersingular elliptic curve and the \((p-1)\)-torsion of its quadratic twist [Cost20].

2 Protocol Design

The B-SIDH protocol structurally mimics the traditional SIDH walk-through but instantiates the operations such that Alice and Bob work in opposite quadratic twists using degrees \(M\) and \(N\), respectively, where \(M |(p+1)\) and \(N | (p-1)\).

2.1 Public Parameters

Unlike traditional SIDH, which requires primes of the restrictive form \(p = f \cdot 2^a 3^b - 1\) (forcing both parties’ smooth degrees to divide \(p+1\)), B-SIDH selects a prime \(p\) such that both \(p+1\) and \(p-1\) are engineered to be smooth, allowing for significantly smaller primes.

The protocol specifies a starting supersingular curve \(E_0 / \mathbb{F}_{p^2}\). It also establishes a basis \(\{P_A, Q_A\}\) for the \(M\)-torsion in \(E_0(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})\) and a basis \(\{P_B, Q_B\}\) for the \(N\)-torsion corresponding to the quadratic twist. Because Montgomery arithmetic is twist-agnostic, coordinates from both sets are processed identically using their \(x\)-coordinates over \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\).

2.2 Alice’s Key Generation

  • Alice chooses two secret integers \((\alpha_A, \beta_A) \in \mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\).
  • She computes her secret point \(S_A = [\alpha_A]P_A + [\beta_A]Q_A\) of order \(M\) from the \((p+1)\)-torsion.
  • She uses \(S_A\) to compute her secret \(M\)-isogeny \(\phi_A : E_0 \rightarrow E_A\), where \(E_A = E_0 / \langle S_A \rangle\). Because \(p+1\) is engineered to be smooth but may contain larger prime factors than traditional SIDH, computing her secret isogeny requires composing walks of multiple prime degrees (\(l\)-isogenies). Evaluating these larger \(l\)-isogenies in practice is accelerated using \(\sqrt{élu}\).
  • During the isogeny computation, she maps Bob’s basis points \(P_B\) and \(Q_B\) through \(\phi_A\).
  • Alice’s Public Key: \(PK_A = (E_A, \phi_A(P_B), \phi_A(Q_B))\).

2.3 Bob’s Key Generation

  • Bob chooses two secret integers \((\alpha_B, \beta_B) \in \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\).
  • He computes his secret point \(S_B = [\alpha_B]P_B + [\beta_B]Q_B\) of order \(N\) from the \((p-1)\)-torsion (on the quadratic twist).
  • He uses \(S_B\) to compute his secret \(N\)-isogeny \(\phi_B : E_0 \rightarrow E_B\), where \(E_B = E_0 / \langle S_B \rangle\). Just like Alice, Bob’s \(N\)-isogeny computation involves larger prime factors from \(p-1\) and applies the same \(\sqrt{élu}\).
  • During his isogeny computation, he maps Alice’s basis points \(P_A\) and \(Q_A\) through \(\phi_B\).
  • Bob’s Public Key: \(PK_B = (E_B, \phi_B(P_A), \phi_B(Q_A))\).

2.4 Shared Secret Computation

  • Alice: Upon receiving \(PK_B\), Alice uses her secret integers to compute a new point \(S'_A = [\alpha_A]\phi_B(P_A) + [\beta_A]\phi_B(Q_A)\) of order \(M\) on curve \(E_B\). She computes the \(M\)-isogeny \(\phi'_A: E_B \rightarrow E_B / \langle S'_A \rangle\).
  • Bob: Upon receiving \(PK_A\), Bob uses his secret integers to compute a new point \(S'_B = [\alpha_B]\phi_A(P_B) + [\beta_B]\phi_A(Q_B)\) of order \(N\) on curve \(E_A\). He computes the \(N\)-isogeny \(\phi'_B: E_A \rightarrow E_A / \langle S'_B \rangle\).
  • Both parties successfully arrive at the same shared secret by computing the j-invariant of their respective final image curves, as \(E_B / \langle S'_A \rangle \cong E_A / \langle S'_B \rangle\).

3 Security Assumptions

B-SIDH relies on different security assumptions compared to standard SIDH, primarily because it alters the nature of the isogeny walks.

  • Multiple Edge Sets: Unlike SIDH, where walks are restricted to a single \(l\)-isogeny graph, B-SIDH requires switching between multiple \(l\)-isogeny graphs. Security assumes that navigating multiple edge sets does not weaken the underlying supersingular isogeny problem, as long as the total number of destination nodes remains comparable to SIDH.

  • Full-Length Walks: The reduction in prime size means that Alice and Bob’s secret walks cover nearly the entire graph (almost full-length), compared to the half-length walks in standard SIDH. The complexity of attacks reamins dictated by the supersingular isogeny graph path finding algorithms.
