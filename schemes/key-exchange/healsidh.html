HealSIDH

Healed Supersingular Isogeny Diffie-Hellman

Published

July 3, 2026

Modified

July 11, 2026

1 Overview

Broken by a polynomial time classical algorithm.

HealSIDH is a statically secure, interactive key exchange scheme designed to mitigate the adaptive attack without resorting to key disclosure (as seen in SIKE) or computationally expensive parallel executions (as seen in \(k\)-SIDH). At its core, HealSIDH introduces a direct key validation mechanism that allows each party to prove the honest generation of their transmitted torsion points [FoPe21]. By operating over a larger prime characteristic of the form \(p = 2^{2a}3^{2b}f - 1\) (where \(2^a \approx 3^b\)) and utilizing a random supersingular starting curve \(E_0\) with an unknown endomorphism ring, the scheme successfully neutralizes both the adaptive attack and improved passive torsion point attacks.

2 Scheme Design

The scheme fundamentally integrates the direct key validation mechanism within the standard SIDH workflow. The design involves canonical basis generation to prevent the leakage of points on the shared secret curve.

2.1 Setup and Public Parameters

  • Prime Characteristic: \(p = 2^{2a}3^{2b}f - 1\) such that \(2^a \approx 3^b\), allowing the required \(2^{2a}\)- and \(3^{2b}\)-torsion points to be defined over \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\).
  • Starting Curve: \(E_0\), a random supersingular elliptic curve defined over \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\) with an unknown endomorphism ring (to thwart torsion point attacks).
  • Torsion Bases:
    • \(E_0[2^{2a}] = \langle P_2, Q_2 \rangle\)
    • \(E_0[3^{2b}] = \langle P_3, Q_3 \rangle\)
  • Scaled Points:
    • \(P_A = [2^a]P_2\), \(Q_A = [2^a]Q_2\) (forming a basis for the \(2^a\)-torsion)
    • \(P_B = [3^b]P_3\), \(Q_B = [3^b]Q_3\) (forming a basis for the \(3^b\)-torsion)

2.2 Key Generation

Alice’s Key Generation:

  1. Secret Key: Alice samples a random integer \(\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}/{2^a}\mathbb{Z}\).
  2. Secret Isogeny: She computes the cyclic isogeny \(\phi_A: E_0 \rightarrow E_A = E_0 / \langle P_A + [\alpha]Q_A \rangle\).
  3. Torsion Image Evaluation: She computes the images \(R_b = \phi_A(P_3)\) and \(S_b = \phi_A(Q_3)\), which will be sent to Bob. She also computes \(\phi_A(P_2)\) and \(\phi_A(Q_2)\).
  4. Canonical Basis Extraction: Alice canonically generates a basis \(\{R_A, S_A\}\) of \(E_A[2^{2a}]\).
  5. Coefficient Calculation: She solves for the coefficients \(e_1, f_1, e_2, f_2 \in \mathbb{Z}_{2^{2a}}\) such that:
    • \(\phi_A(P_2) = [e_1]R_A + [f_1]S_A\)
    • \(\phi_A(Q_2) = [e_2]R_A + [f_2]S_A\)
  6. Keys: Her public key is \((E_A, R_b, S_b)\) and her secret key is the tuple \((\alpha, e_1, f_1, e_2, f_2)\).

Bob’s Key Generation:

  1. Secret Key: Bob samples a random integer \(\beta \in \mathbb{Z}/{3^b}\mathbb{Z}\).
  2. Secret Isogeny: He computes the cyclic isogeny \(\phi_B: E_0 \rightarrow E_B = E_0 / \langle P_B + [\beta]Q_B \rangle\).
  3. Torsion Image Evaluation: He computes the images \(R_a = \phi_B(P_2)\) and \(S_a = \phi_B(Q_2)\), which will be sent to Alice. He also computes \(\phi_B(P_3)\) and \(\phi_B(Q_3)\).
  4. Canonical Basis Extraction: Bob canonically generates a basis \(\{R_B, S_B\}\) of \(E_B[3^{2b}]\).
  5. Coefficient Calculation: He solves for the coefficients \(g_1, h_1, g_2, h_2 \in \mathbb{Z}_{3^{2b}}\) such that:
    • \(\phi_B(P_3) = [g_1]R_B + [h_1]S_B\)
    • \(\phi_B(Q_3) = [g_2]R_B + [h_2]S_B\)
  6. Keys: His public key is \((E_B, R_a, S_a)\) and his secret key is the tuple \((\beta, g_1, h_1, g_2, h_2)\).

2.3 Key Exchange and Validation Phase

To establish a shared secret, the parties compute their second-stage isogenies, evaluate them on the canonical bases, and perform cross-verification checks to ensure honesty.

  1. Bob’s Second Phase & Transmission:
    • Bob computes his second isogeny \(\phi'_B: E_A \rightarrow E_{AB} = E_A / \langle [3^b]R_b + [\beta][3^b]S_b \rangle\).
    • He canonically generates a basis \(\{R_{AB}, S_{AB}\}\) for \(E_{AB}[2^{2a}]\).
    • He computes the image of Alice’s canonical basis under \(\phi'_B\), extracting coefficients \(e_3, f_3, e_4, f_4 \in \mathbb{Z}_{2^{2a}}\) such that:
      • \(\phi'_B(R_A) = [e_3]R_{AB} + [f_3]S_{AB}\)
      • \(\phi'_B(S_A) = [e_4]R_{AB} + [f_4]S_{AB}\)
    • Bob transmits the coefficients \((e_3, f_3, e_4, f_4)\) to Alice.
  2. Alice’s Second Phase & Transmission:
    • Alice computes her second isogeny \(\phi'_A: E_B \rightarrow E_{BA} = E_B / \langle [2^a]R_a + [\alpha][2^a]S_a \rangle\).
    • She canonically generates a basis \(\{R_{BA}, S_{BA}\}\) for \(E_{BA}[3^{2b}]\).
    • She computes the image of Bob’s canonical basis under \(\phi'_A\), extracting coefficients \(g_3, h_3, g_4, h_4 \in \mathbb{Z}_{3^{2b}}\) such that:
      • \(\phi'_A(R_B) = [g_3]R_{BA} + [h_3]S_{BA}\)
      • \(\phi'_A(S_B) = [g_4]R_{BA} + [h_4]S_{BA}\)
    • Alice transmits the coefficients \((g_3, h_3, g_4, h_4)\) to Bob.
  3. Alice’s Validation Checks:
    • She canonically generates the basis \(\{R_{AB}, S_{AB}\}\) for \(E_{BA}[2^{2a}]\) (since \(E_{BA} \cong E_{AB}\)).
    • She sets up expected points based on Bob’s transmitted coefficients:
      • \(R_{ab} = [e_3]R_{AB} + [f_3]S_{AB}\)
      • \(S_{ab} = [e_4]R_{AB} + [f_4]S_{AB}\)
    • Alice verifies Bob’s honesty by checking:
      1. The Weil pairing: \(e_{2^{2a}}(R_a, S_a) \stackrel{?}{=} e_{2^{2a}}(P_2, Q_2)^{3^b}\)
      2. Coefficient validation 1: \(\phi'_A(R_a) \stackrel{?}{=} [e_1]R_{ab} + [f_1]S_{ab}\)
      3. Coefficient validation 2: \(\phi'_A(S_a) \stackrel{?}{=} [e_2]R_{ab} + [f_2]S_{ab}\)
    • If all checks pass, she accepts \(j(E_{BA})\) as the shared secret.
  4. Bob’s Validation Checks:
    • He canonically generates the basis \(\{R_{BA}, S_{BA}\}\) for \(E_{AB}[3^{2b}]\).
    • He sets up expected points based on Alice’s transmitted coefficients:
      • \(R_{ba} = [g_3]R_{BA} + [h_3]S_{BA}\)
      • \(S_{ba} = [g_4]R_{BA} + [h_4]S_{BA}\)
    • Bob verifies Alice’s honesty by checking:
      1. The Weil pairing: \(e_{3^{2b}}(R_b, S_b) \stackrel{?}{=} e_{3^{2b}}(P_3, Q_3)^{2^a}\)
      2. Coefficient validation 1: \(\phi'_B(R_b) \stackrel{?}{=} [g_1]R_{ba} + [h_1]S_{ba}\)
      3. Coefficient validation 2: \(\phi'_B(S_b) \stackrel{?}{=} [g_2]R_{ba} + [h_2]S_{ba}\)
    • If all checks pass, he accepts \(j(E_{AB})\) as the shared secret. Correctness dictates that \(j(E_{AB}) = j(E_{BA})\).

3 Security Assumptions

The security of HealSIDH relies on a combination of new computational assumptions introduced in the paper and the mitigation of previously known vulnerabilities in SIDH-based schemes.

  • HealSIDH Computational Diffie-Hellman Problem (HealSIDH-CDHP): The security of the key exchange against shared secret recovery relies on a new variant of the Supersingular Isogeny Computational Diffie-Hellman Problem (SSICDHP). This assumption posits that given the public parameters, public keys, and the validation coefficients, it is computationally intractable for an adversary to compute the shared curve \(E_{AB}\).

  • Indistinguishability of Canonical Basis Images: For the semantic security (IND-CPA) of derived encryption schemes like SHealS, the authors formalize an assumption stating that the validation coefficients (representing the mapping of the canonical basis through the second-stage isogeny) are computationally indistinguishable from coefficients derived from a uniformly random basis of \(E_{AB}[2^{2a}]\) sharing the same Weil pairing value.

  • Mitigation of Adaptive Attacks: The direct key validation methodology mathematically bounds a malicious participant, enforcing that any deviation in forming the torsion images will fail the pairing and coefficient checks.

  • Resistance to Passive Torsion Point Attacks: Because HealSIDH exposes the actions of the secret isogeny on larger torsion groups, it would typically be susceptible to the passive torsion point attacks. To counter this, HealSIDH borrows the established mitigation of utilizing a starting curve \(E_0\) with an unknown endomorphism ring, preventing the exploitation of known endomorphisms to recover the secret isogeny.
